# How to Start a Now We See You Chapter at Your School

This guide is for students, teachers, or community members who want to bring *Now We See You* to a new school.

Everything here is based on how the first chapter was built at Lake Washington High School, then adapted into a process another school can repeat.

> **What you need before you start:** one or two committed student leads, a supportive staff advisor, and a willingness to spend more time listening than publishing. The project is built on consent, not speed.

---

![Roles and Workflow](./assets/roles-and-nomination-workflow-jul2026.png)

The diagram above shows the original July 2026 role design. The final workflow changed slightly during implementation. In particular, publishing now stays with an administrator, and artists and photographers can start their work before the journalist creates the written profile.

## 1. Set up your club roles

Structure your chapter as a club with defined roles, not a loose group of volunteers.

That is what lets the chapter work without one person becoming the bottleneck.

- **Journalist** - leads interviews, writes the story, and follows the profile through review.
- **Photographer** - takes approved reference and profile photographs.
- **Artist** - creates the portrait or visual work for the profile.
- **Community Outreach** - helps share published work, create flyers, and connect the project back to the school community.
- **School administrator** - reviews nominations, manages access, and makes the final publishing decision.

You do not need a different person for every role on day one.

One student can hold more than one role. That is especially useful in a new chapter where the team is still small.

The platform supports overlapping roles rather than assuming every chapter starts with a full club.

---

## 2. Secure a staff advisor and school support

Before featuring anyone, talk with your school.

A principal, activities coordinator, teacher, counselor, or communications lead can help with scheduling, access, school policy, and introducing the project to staff.

Explain that:

- the project is a long-term, consent-based archive, not a social media campaign
- being nominated does not mean someone automatically becomes public
- every featured staff member reviews what is being shared
- participants can ask for changes or ask for their profile to be removed
- student work is separated into defined roles
- an administrator keeps the final publishing permission

It is useful to keep a written record of the school's support and the staff member who is advising the chapter.

---

## 3. Get your school added to the platform

Now We See You now supports multiple school chapters inside the same platform.

A new chapter does not need a separate website.

The global administrator creates the school in the Now We See You dashboard and assigns the school's first administrator.

That creates:

- the school record
- the first school administrator
- the chapter's private admin space

The new school does not automatically become public just because it has been created.

There are three separate stages:

1. The school exists in the platform.
2. The school chooses to accept public nominations.
3. The school publishes its first profile and appears in the public Galleries directory.

This keeps a chapter from looking active before the students and school are actually ready.

---

## 4. Add your club members

The school administrator uses **Manage Roles** to add students to the chapter.

Available roles are:

```text
Journalist
Photographer
Artist
Community Outreach
```

A role is tied to one school.

Being a Journalist at one school does not give access to another school's nominations or profiles.

Students sign in through the same **Club & Admin Access** page as administrators.

Journalists, photographers, and artists are routed to their Club Dashboard.

Community Outreach receives access to the Flyer Generator for published profiles without receiving the full administrator dashboard.

---

## 5. Decide when to open public nominations

Each school controls whether it appears in the public **Nominate Someone** form.

The administrator can turn **Public Nominations** on or off in the school settings.

This is useful during setup.

A chapter can create its team and prepare its process without immediately accepting nominations from the community.

Once the school is ready, turn nominations on.

---

## 6. Define your first featured roles

Pick a small group of people to begin with.

The first chapter focused on classified and support staff, especially people whose work is essential to the school but rarely recognized publicly.

Good candidates include:

- custodians and maintenance staff
- food service workers
- bookkeepers and front-office staff
- paraeducators and instructional assistants
- bus drivers
- library technicians
- other staff members who work behind the scenes

Start with **one to three people** for your pilot.

A small launch gives you room to learn the process before trying to scale it.

---

## 7. Nominate and ask for consent

Community members can submit nominations through the public nomination page.

A nomination asks who the person is, what they do, why they should be recognized, and enough contact information for the chapter to follow up.

A nomination starts as:

```text
pending
```

A school administrator reviews it.

If the nomination is appropriate and the chapter is ready to work on it, the administrator can approve it and assign a Journalist, Photographer, and Artist.

The status becomes:

```text
approved
```

Approval is not the same as consent to publish.

Consent still happens with the person being featured.

The process should cover:

1. **Consent to participate in the project.**
2. **Consent to be interviewed, photographed, or drawn.**
3. **Consent to publish the specific story, quotes, photographs, and artwork being used.**
4. **Consent to keep a public profile and appreciation wall available on the site.**

Let them say no.

Let them take time to decide.

Let them review the final draft before anything goes live.

Do not treat a nomination as permission to publish about someone.

---

## 8. Start the creative work

The artist and photographer do not have to wait for the journalist to finish the interview or create the profile.

This was one of the changes made after testing the club workflow.

Creative uploads are tied to the nomination first.

That means:

- the Artist can upload portrait work while the nomination is approved
- the Photographer can upload approved photographs while the nomination is approved
- the Journalist can work on the interview and written profile at the same time

This matches the way the work happens in real life.

A portrait might be finished before the story.

A photograph might already exist before the interview.

The platform does not force those jobs into an artificial order.

---

## 9. Collect the story

Schedule a 20 to 40 minute interview in a quiet place.

Good questions to start with:

- What is your official job title, and what does a typical day look like?
- How long have you worked here, and what did you do before?
- What is something most students or staff do not know about your work?
- What makes you feel seen or appreciated at school?
- Is there anything you want the school community to understand about your role?

Record audio only with permission.

Take notes.

Try to preserve the person's own voice rather than turning the profile into a formal biography.

Share the draft with the person being featured and ask for corrections.

---

## 10. Create the portrait and visual identity

Each profile should have a strong visual element.

The original chapter uses hand-drawn portraits, especially charcoal work, but another chapter does not need to copy the exact medium.

An artist can work in charcoal, ink, watercolor, digital painting, or another medium that fits their own ability.

What matters is that the artwork is created thoughtfully for the person being recognized.

Consistency across a chapter can come from composition, presentation, cropping, and display style rather than forcing every artist to work exactly the same way.

If you use photography, make sure the person is comfortable with both the photograph and the setting.

The point is to show the person, not reduce them to their uniform or job.

---

## 11. Create the written profile

The assigned Journalist opens the nomination in the Club Dashboard.

The first saved write-up creates the linked draft profile.

At that point the nomination moves to:

```text
in_progress
```

The Journalist can work with:

- name
- role
- department
- public profile slug
- featured quote
- full story
- optional Staff Reflection quote
- optional Staff Reflection video URL
- optional reflection recording date

The profile remains a draft.

The Journalist can edit it, but cannot publish it.

That separation is intentional.

The person writing the public story should not automatically be the person making the final decision to put it live.

---

## 12. Add the QR code

Once the draft profile exists and its public slug has been saved, the Journalist can generate the tracked profile QR code.

The QR does not point straight to the profile page.

It points through a redirect that can log the scan and send the visitor to:

```text
https://nowweseeyou.org/gallery/<profile-slug>
```

This makes the QR more durable than printing the profile destination directly.

If the Journalist changes the profile slug, they should save the change before generating or refreshing the QR.

The physical QR and the destination behind it should be treated as separate things.

That lesson came directly from the first Brad Fisher flyer, where an early QR pointed to the original Google Sites version of the project.

---

## 13. Review and publish

Before publication, the administrator reviews the completed work.

Check:

- the participant has reviewed the profile
- the story is accurate
- the approved portrait and photographs are attached
- the public slug is correct
- the QR points to the intended profile
- anything private or unnecessary has been removed

The administrator then publishes the profile.

The active workflow is:

```text
pending
   |
approved
   |
in_progress
   |
published
```

The database also contains some earlier status values from the first workflow design, but these are the main stages used by the current chapter process.

When the linked profile becomes published, the nomination is also synchronized to `published`.

---

## 14. Public chapter launch

A school appears in the public Galleries directory after it has at least one published profile.

The public structure is:

```text
/galleries
/galleries/<school>
/gallery/<profile>
```

The Galleries page lists active chapters with published work.

Each school has its own gallery.

Individual staff profile URLs stay separate and stable because physical QR codes may already point to them.

---

## 15. Launch with a small, respectful event

A launch does not need to be loud.

Some ideas that worked well:

- a hallway display with the portrait, a short quote, and the QR code
- a school announcement introducing the person and inviting students to read their story
- an art exhibition or community display
- a handwritten thank-you board near the portrait
- sharing the profile through a school newsletter or club page

Make sure the featured person knows where their portrait or profile will appear and is comfortable with the final display.

Brad Fisher's portrait from the first chapter was later displayed at the Kirkland Arts Center Youth Art Showcase.

That showed me that the same profile and QR system could work outside the school building too.

---

## 16. Moderate the appreciation wall

Public appreciation is powerful, but it needs moderation.

A good starting policy is:

- keep messages kind, specific, and school-appropriate
- do not allow jokes at the person's expense
- do not allow gossip
- do not allow romantic comments
- do not publish personal contact information
- give the featured person the ability to ask for a message to be removed

Now We See You uses automated moderation before appreciation messages become public.

The moderation system is a safeguard, not a replacement for judgment.

If something gets through that should not be public, an administrator can still remove it.

---

## 17. Community Outreach after publishing

Community Outreach starts with published work.

They can:

- generate a printable flyer for a published profile
- help place or distribute the flyer
- share the public story through appropriate school channels
- invite the featured person to an event or conversation if they are interested
- help the chapter build relationships with school or community organizations

Community Outreach does not control publication.

The role gets access to the published work it needs without receiving the rest of the administrator dashboard.

---

## 18. Keep the chapter alive

After the first launch, the harder part is consistency.

Plan to:

- feature someone new on a schedule the team can actually maintain
- keep a shared calendar of interviews, review dates, art deadlines, and launches
- keep consent records and approved drafts organized
- train new students before older members graduate
- let students hold more than one role when the chapter is small
- bring in more people as the workload grows
- track profile views and QR scans
- ask featured staff how the process felt from their side

Do not measure the chapter only by how many profiles it publishes.

A smaller number of profiles done respectfully is better than rushing through people just to increase a count.

---

## The one rule that matters most

> **Nothing is published without the person's clear, informed, revocable consent.**

That rule matters more than the software, the portrait style, the QR code, or the number of chapters.

If you are starting a new chapter, begin with one person.

Ask permission to talk.

Then listen.
